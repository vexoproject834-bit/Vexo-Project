01 — About VEXO

A material question hiding in a waste stream.

VEXO investigates whether whole groundwater-treatment sludge can be engineered into a multifunctional material for gas-phase ozone removal. Instead of extracting one component and rebuilding a separate catalyst, it explores keeping the native multicomponent sludge matrix and asks how its composition, surface properties, humidity response and reuse behavior relate to ozone-removal performance.

Background

Waste or resource?

Treatment residuals are usually managed as waste. VEXO examines whether their mineral and carbon-containing makeup could carry functional value.

Hypothesis

Whole matrix

The combined constituents may act together in ways an isolated component would not. This is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.

Scope

Six disciplines

Environmental engineering, material science, air purification, water treatment, waste valorization, sustainable innovation.

02 — The problem

Two environmental challenges, one possible link.

Established background

Ground-level ozone

Ozone near the surface is a recognized air pollutant that harms respiratory health and vegetation. Reducing exposure, including indoors and in ventilation streams, is an active area of air-quality work.

Established background

Conventional materials

Many ozone-removal materials depend on purpose-made catalysts or sorbents whose production, cost and end-of-life add their own burden.

Established background

Sludge management

Water-treatment plants generate sludge continuously. Handling, transport and disposal are ongoing operational and environmental challenges.

03 — Our innovation

Keep the matrix. Don’t isolate one component.

VEXO’s central idea is to retain the whole multicomponent sludge matrix by default, and to engineer it only when evidence justifies it.

Conventional approach

Component extraction

  • Select one target component
  • Separate and purify it
  • Rebuild a separate catalyst
  • Additional processing steps
VEXO approach

Whole-matrix evaluation

  • Retain the native sludge matrix
  • Characterize what it actually contains
  • Engineer only when justified
  • Test ozone removal, humidity response and reuse

05 — The science behind VEXO

What each constituent might contribute.

The roles below come from the general literature. VEXO does not claim any of them are confirmed in our samples; that requires characterization and testing.
Potential

Manganese-containing phases

Manganese oxides are widely reported in the literature as ozone-decomposition catalysts. Whether such phases exist in this sludge, and in what form, is to be determined.

Potential

Carbon-containing fractions

Carbonaceous material may influence surface area, porosity and adsorption behavior. It might also change under ozone exposure, which needs to be assessed.

Potential

Iron-containing phases

Iron (oxy)hydroxides and oxides can offer reactive surface sites and may interact with moisture. Their actual role here is unverified.

Potential

Other mineral phases

Calcium-, silicon- and aluminium-bearing minerals may shape structure, porosity and water uptake, and might influence the others.

06 — Research methodology

Seven ways of asking the material questions.

XRD

Identifies crystalline phases present in the material.

SEM-EDS

Reveals morphology and the distribution of elements across particles.

BET / porosity

Measures specific surface area and pore structure.

XPS (if available)

Probes surface elemental composition and oxidation states.

Ozone-removal evaluation

Compares ozone concentration entering and leaving a material bed.

Humidity response

Examines how moisture changes ozone-removal behavior.

Reuse & regeneration

Tests whether performance holds over repeated cycles.

08 — Research & validation

A proposed framework. No results yet.

Status: every experiment below is proposed. No experimental results, removal efficiencies or measurements are reported on this site. They will be added only when data exists.

Research question

Can whole groundwater-treatment sludge be engineered into a multifunctional material for gas-phase ozone removal, and how do its composition, surface properties, humidity response and reuse behavior relate to performance?

Central hypothesis

Retaining the native multicomponent matrix may support ozone removal through the combined effects of its constituents. This is a hypothesis to be experimentally assessed.

Independent variables

Material condition (as-received vs engineered, when justified) and relative humidity.

Dependent variables

Ozone-removal efficiency and change in performance across repeated cycles.

Controlled variables

Airflow rate, inlet ozone concentration, temperature, bed mass and bed geometry.

Data analysis

Replicate runs per group, with removal efficiency calculated from inlet and outlet concentrations and compared between groups.

Removal Efficiency (%) = [(Cin − Cout) / Cin] × 100
